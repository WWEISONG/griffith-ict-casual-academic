# 5. Evaluation

*How we will know whether the program worked — and how we would know if it did not.*

---

## The standard to hold it to

A program that cannot fail its own evaluation is not being evaluated. The
measures below are chosen so that a genuinely unsuccessful pilot would show up
as unsuccessful.

The pilot runs for **one trimester**. It is reviewed against these measures
before any commitment to continue.

---

## Measures

### 1. Coverage — did we reach the people we needed to?

| Measure | Source | Target |
|---|---|---|
| First-time tutors who attended before teaching | Attendance record | **≥ 90%** |
| Courses that recruited through the portal | Portal | **≥ 60%** of courses appointing tutors |
| Candidates in the pool at trimester start | Portal | **≥ 40** |
| Courses appointing with no candidate in the pool | Portal | Falling trimester on trimester |

The last one is the honest measure of whether the pool is real. A large pool
that convenors bypass has failed.

### 2. Participant judgement — was it worth their three hours?

Collected at the end of the workshop, anonymously, four questions:

- I feel prepared to run my first tutorial *(1–5)*
- The workshop was a good use of three hours *(1–5)*
- What was most useful? *(free text)*
- What should we drop or change? *(free text)*

**Target: mean ≥ 4.0 on both scales.** Free-text responses matter more than the
numbers and go straight into the next trimester's design.

### 3. Convenor judgement — were the tutors better?

A three-question survey to convenors at the end of the trimester:

- Were your first-time tutors prepared for their first session compared with
  previous trimesters? *(better / same / worse / no basis to compare)*
- How much of your own time went into preparing them? *(more / same / less)*
- Would you recruit through the portal again? *(yes / no — why?)*

**This is the measure that decides continuation.** If convenors say "same" and
"no", the program has not earned its cost, whatever the participants said on the
day.

### 4. Student experience — the point of all of it

Griffith's existing student evaluation instruments already ask about teaching
quality in tutorials. Compare, for courses with first-time tutors:

- Tutorial-related items, this trimester against the same course previously

**Treated as indicative, not decisive.** Cohorts differ, response rates are low,
and a single trimester cannot isolate the effect of a three-hour workshop. It is
reported because it is the outcome that actually matters, not because one
trimester can prove anything about it.

### 5. Retention — do good tutors come back?

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Tutors who taught this trimester and register for the next | **≥ 60%** |
| Tutors returning to a course they have taught before | Rising |

Retention is the quiet measure of whether the School is a decent place to be a
casual academic. Poor retention with good satisfaction scores means something is
wrong after the workshop, not during it.

---

## Reporting

A **two-page report to the Head of School** at the end of each trimester:

1. Coverage figures
2. Workshop feedback, with the free-text themes
3. Convenor feedback
4. What is changing next trimester, and why
5. Anything that needs a decision

---

## What would make this a failure

Stated in advance, so the conclusion cannot be moved afterwards:

- **Convenors bypass the portal.** If most appointments happen outside it, the
  pool is not doing its job and the process should be redesigned around what
  convenors actually do.
- **Convenors report no difference.** If the tutors are no better prepared, the
  three hours are not earning their cost and the workshop content is wrong.
- **Attendance is low.** If first-time tutors do not come, either the
  requirement is not real or the timing is wrong.
- **It depends on one person.** If the program cannot run without its author, it
  is not a School process — it is a hobby with a budget. The materials and run
  sheet exist so that somebody else can deliver it, and the pilot should test
  that by having a second person deliver at least one segment.

---

## After the pilot

If the pilot succeeds, three extensions are worth considering, in this order:

1. **A mid-trimester marking session**, in Week 4, when marking becomes real and
   the calibration exercise has something concrete to work on.
2. **Peer observation** — experienced tutors observing new ones once, briefly,
   developmentally.
3. **A recognised progression** from tutor to senior tutor, with the senior
   tutors running segments of the workshop. This costs nothing and gives the
   School a way to retain its best casual academics, which it currently lacks.
