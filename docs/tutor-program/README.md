# Casual Academic Development Program

**School of Information and Communication Technology, Griffith University**
Proposal for a standardised tutor recruitment, training and development cycle.

## The problem in one paragraph

The School appoints casual academics — tutors, demonstrators and markers — every
trimester, across 187 courses. Recruitment happens through individual convenors'
memory and email. Once appointed, a new tutor's preparation for the classroom
depends entirely on which convenor hired them. Some receive a thorough briefing;
some are told the room number. There is no School-level standard for what a
tutor is expected to be able to do before they stand in front of students, and
no record of who has been prepared and how.

This proposal closes both halves of that gap: **a system that makes tutors
findable**, and **a program that makes them ready**.

## What is being proposed

**One cycle, run every trimester, with a named owner.**

```
Results released          Students with strong results in a course are invited
        │                 to register interest in tutoring it next trimester
        ▼
Candidates apply          Casual Academic Portal — one standing application,
        │                 teaching history, ranked course preferences
        ▼
Convenors select          Search the pool by course: who has taught it, who has
        │                 applied for it. Contact directly.
        ▼
School confirms           Appointments recorded; new tutors identified
        │
        ▼
Week 0 workshop           Three hours, before teaching starts. Mandatory for
        │                 first-time tutors, School-run.
        ▼
Teaching                  With a defined tutorial model, a marking standard and
        │                 a conduct expectation everyone has been told
        ▼
Close-out                 Convenor confirms completion; the tutor's record
                          carries forward to the next trimester
```

Two things make this different from what happens now. First, the invitation to
tutor is **proactive and merit-based** — strong students are asked, rather than
left to discover the opportunity. Second, training is **a School standard, not a
convenor's discretion**.

---

## Why the School should own this

Griffith requires casual academics to complete mandatory compliance training —
work health and safety, integrity, and the systems for recording their hours.
That training is necessary and it is not teaching preparation. Nothing in it
tells a new tutor how to run the first twenty minutes of a lab, what to do when
a student asks for the answer, or how to mark forty submissions consistently
with three other markers.

That knowledge is discipline-specific. A computing tutorial has a shape a law
tutorial does not: a concept review, then supported practice at a keyboard,
where the tutor's main skill is helping someone debug their own thinking rather
than fixing their code. No central program can teach that. The School can.

## Cost

| | |
|---|---|
| Facilitator | 3 hours, plus preparation. Existing academic workload. |
| Senior tutor panel | 3 × 1 hour |
| Participant time | 3 hours per new tutor |
| Room | One computer lab, Week 0 |
| Materials | Prepared once, reused each trimester |

For a cohort of twenty new tutors, the recurring cost is approximately sixty
hours of casual time per trimester. Set against it: fewer failed tutorials in
weeks 1–3, fewer marking disputes, fewer escalations to convenors, and a tutor
cohort the School can actually vouch for.

## What is asked of the Head of School

1. **Endorse the cycle** in [2. The trimester cycle](02-process.md) as School practice.
2. **Endorse attendance** at the Week 0 workshop as an expectation for first-time tutors.
3. **Confirm the owner** of the program — proposed: Wei Song, as Casual Academic Coordinator.
4. **Agree the pilot**: run it once, for one trimester, and review against
   [5. Evaluation](05-evaluation.md) before committing to it permanently.
