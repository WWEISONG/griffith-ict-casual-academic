# 1. Gap analysis

*What Griffith already provides for casual academics, and what it does not.*

---

## How this was checked, and its limits

This review is based on **publicly accessible Griffith University sources** —
the University website, published procedure documents, and the staff portal
pages that are visible without authentication — searched in September 2026.

**It cannot see behind the staff intranet.** Griffith runs training through
Canvas and the secure staff portal, and a School-level or Group-level program
that is not publicly listed would not appear here. Two checks are therefore
needed before this proposal is put to a decision:

1. **Ask the ICT School Manager** whether any School-level teaching induction
   for casual academics has run previously.
2. **Ask Griffith Learning Futures** (or the equivalent central teaching unit)
   whether a discipline-specific sessional teaching program exists that ICT
   could adopt rather than build.

If either turns one up, the right move is to adopt or extend it, not duplicate
it. That is a better outcome than building something new, and it should be
established before, not after, this is presented.

---

## What Griffith does provide

| Provision | What it covers | What it does not |
|---|---|---|
| **Mandatory onboarding** — required of all employees including casual academics, delivered through Canvas | Work health and safety, integrity and conduct, privacy, equity and inclusion | Anything about teaching |
| **Casual Staff Time Recording Procedure training** | Timesheets, the sessional timetabling process, how to be paid correctly | Anything about teaching |
| **"How to engage sessional staff"** guidance | The engagement process, for the *hiring* academic | Preparation of the person hired |
| **Academic Staff Career Development**, educational assistance schemes | Continuing academic staff development | Casual academics, who are typically ineligible or not targeted |
| **Tutoring for Success** (Gumurrii) | Tutor development — for tutors supporting Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students | A different program, a different cohort, a different purpose |

The pattern is clear and unsurprising: Griffith's casual academic provision is
**compliance and payroll**. It is well organised, and it answers the questions
the University must answer as an employer.

None of it prepares somebody to teach.

---

## What is missing

A casual academic appointed to a Griffith ICT course today receives:

- Compliance modules, on Canvas, about workplace obligations
- Instructions on submitting a timesheet
- Whatever their course convenor chooses to tell them

The third item is the whole of their teaching preparation, and it varies from a
detailed handover to a room number. There is:

- **No School standard** for what a tutor should be able to do before teaching
- **No shared model** of how an ICT tutorial or lab is run
- **No marking calibration** across tutors on the same course
- **No stated conduct expectation** beyond the University's general code
- **No record** of who has been prepared, so nothing carries between trimesters
- **No route** for experienced tutors to pass on what they know

---

## What is normal practice elsewhere

Discipline-level induction for casual teaching staff is established practice in
Australian computing schools. The recurring shape is:

- A **tutor induction session each term**, covering tutorials, labs and help
  sessions, marking, and the teaching tools in use
- An **additional session in Week 0 for first-time** tutors and lab
  demonstrators
- **Attendance expected** of new tutors; experienced tutors may attend
- Supported by a persistent written guide covering everything from teaching
  practice to the administrative steps of being employed
- Recruitment runs through a **dedicated School tutor-selection system**
  alongside the central HR system

The structural point: **selection and preparation are one pipeline with one
owner**, rather than an HR process followed by an individual convenor's
improvisation.

School- and Faculty-level sessional teaching development runs at Flinders and
ANU, among others. This proposal is not an innovation — it is standard practice
that the School of ICT does not yet have.

---

## The opportunity

Two observations follow.

**First, the gap is real and it is at School level.** Griffith covers its
obligations as an employer thoroughly. It does not cover the School's interest
in what happens in the tutorial room, because that is not a central function.
ICT is the only body that can close it.

**Second, ICT can now close it cheaply.** The recruitment system already exists
and is deployed: it holds every candidate, what they have taught, and what they
have asked to teach. The missing half is a training step and an owner, which is
one workshop and one named coordinator.

The two halves reinforce each other. The system identifies who the new tutors
are; the program prepares them; and their record — courses taught, training
completed — accumulates in the system and carries into the next trimester,
which is precisely the institutional memory the School currently lacks.
